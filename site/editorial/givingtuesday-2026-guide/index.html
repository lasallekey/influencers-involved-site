GivingTuesday 2026: What It Is and How You Can Take Part

GivingTuesday takes place on Tuesday, December 1, 2026. Whether you can donate, volunteer or simply help a good organization find its people, there is room for you.

By Influencers Involved

You may already have a cause in mind for GivingTuesday. Or you may have heard the name and wondered what, exactly, you're meant to do.

The answer is refreshingly open. GivingTuesday 2026 is Tuesday, December 1, a day when people around the world choose to give in ways that make sense to them: money, time, supplies, skills, attention or ordinary help for someone nearby. There is no universal donation target and no single charity to support. GivingTuesday's official participation guide offers ideas for individuals and communities.

Where did GivingTuesday come from?

GivingTuesday began in 2012 at New York City's 92nd Street Y. It has since grown into an independent nonprofit and a global movement, but its starting idea remains simple: make a little more space for generosity in everyday life. You can read the history on GivingTuesday's own website.

For some people, the day is an opportunity to make their annual charitable gift. Others use it to sign up for their first volunteer shift, organize a neighborhood collection or draw attention to an issue that isn't getting enough notice.

It's also a useful date to ask a question that can get lost during busy fundraising seasons: what does an organization actually need?

How to participate on December 1

Give directly to an organization you trust. If you've followed a food bank, shelter or community program for years, start there. Visit its official website, check what its current campaign supports, and use the donation route the organization itself publishes. Some campaigns offer matching donations for a specified day or up to a stated cap; read the terms rather than assuming every gift is matched.

Offer something other than money. A local nonprofit may need volunteers, delivery help, donated supplies or people with specific professional skills. Ask before collecting items. An enthusiastic carload of things a charity cannot store is less helpful than a short list of supplies it requested.

Bring someone along. A school club, family, workplace or group of friends can choose a cause together. One person might donate, another might volunteer and a third might help with communications. Shared participation doesn't have to mean everyone contributes in the same way.

Make a useful introduction. Know a friend who loves animals, a colleague interested in mentoring or a neighbor who cares about hunger relief? Send them a specific organization's current campaign and explain, in your own words, why you thought of them. A personal introduction is often more welcome than a generic appeal.

Share accurate information. If you post about a nonprofit, link to its official campaign. Say what the organization actually does, what it is asking for and why you care. Avoid unverified fundraising totals or dramatic stories you haven't checked.

What if you don't have money to give?

You can still participate. GivingTuesday explicitly welcomes gifts of time, goods, voice and money. Helping a neighbor, giving blood where eligible, volunteering or amplifying a local need can be meaningful in its own right.

If you choose to share a nonprofit's fundraiser, the best post may be a modest one: name the organization, describe one concrete thing it does, and direct people to learn more. Readers should feel free to decide for themselves.

A particular opportunity for creators

People follow creators for all sorts of reasons: practical advice, humor, entertainment, specialized knowledge and the comfort of a familiar voice. That relationship can help someone discover a cause they might otherwise miss.

A cooking creator might highlight a community pantry. A pet-care creator might explain what a rescue needs before accepting donated supplies. A local lifestyle account might share a nearby volunteer opportunity. None requires a huge audience or a professionally produced fundraising video.

We put together a practical GivingTuesday guide for content creators for anyone wondering how to approach that responsibly. If you're still choosing an organization, our charity-selection checklist can help.

What happens on December 2?

GivingTuesday is a date, not an expiration date for caring. If an organization captures your attention, follow its work after the campaign. Consider volunteering regularly, learning more about its needs or making a future gift when it works for you.

At Influencers Involved, we also invite eligible social media creators to explore the 1% Influence Challenge: a voluntary pledge to give 1% of creator income directly to a legitimate charity of the creator's choosing, on a schedule the creator sets. Signing up recognizes a commitment, not proof that a donation has already been made. Public posting and inviting peers are optional.

GivingTuesday can be a good beginning. The cause you choose—and what you do next—belongs to you.
