At Save The Animals Foundation, GivingTuesday Is About Being Ready for Just One More

Rex and Bubba, two animals helped by Cincinnati's Save The Animals Foundation, are at the center of its 2026 GivingTuesday appeal.

By Influencers InvolvedPublished November 20, 2026

It's easy to understand an animal rescue through the happy ending: the adoption photo, the new family, the dog finally asleep on a sofa.

A shelter also has to be ready before that ending is possible. There is an animal arriving unexpectedly, a medical problem to address, a space to prepare, and someone who has to show up again tomorrow morning.

That is the idea behind #JustOneMore, the Save The Animals Foundation (STAF) campaign for GivingTuesday, December 1, 2026. The Cincinnati organization is asking supporters to help it remain ready for another dog or cat who needs care. Its current GivingTuesday page introduces two rescued animals, Rex and Bubba, as campaign spokesanimals.

Who is Save The Animals Foundation?

STAF describes itself as an all-volunteer, no-kill sanctuary and rescue for cats and dogs in the Greater Cincinnati area. The organization says its volunteers provide daily care, veterinary support, rehabilitation and help with adoption, as well as longer-term sanctuary for animals who need it. Its mission and background explain a model built around sustained, hands-on care.

That detail matters to the campaign. An animal's needs don't arrive neatly in time for a fundraiser. Food, clean space, treatment and human attention continue well after a big giving day has passed.

Rex: a dog who needed someone to say yes

According to STAF's November 8 campaign announcement, Rex was discovered tied up and abandoned in a park. STAF took him in. The charity now describes him as affectionate, spirited and full of personality.

We haven't met Rex and are relying on STAF's own account of his rescue. Still, the reason the organization chose to feature him is easy to understand. The moment a dog is found abandoned is the moment a rescue group may have to decide whether it has the capacity to help.

A campaign called #JustOneMore puts that decision at the center rather than assuming there will always be room.

Bubba: a senior cat facing an unexpected change

Bubba's story is different. STAF says he lost his home when his owner became ill. The organization describes him as a senior cat who welcomes volunteers and shares his space with other cats while he waits for a new home.

For older animals, a change in circumstances can be especially hard. Bubba's story points to another part of rescue work: there isn't always a villain or a dramatic emergency. Sometimes people and pets face a situation they never intended, and a shelter becomes the place that helps them through it.

The official account and photos of both animals are on STAF's campaign announcement.

What is the 2026 GivingTuesday goal?

STAF's campaign page displays a $100,000 fundraising goal and encourages donations in support of #JustOneMore. The organization says gifts help provide food, shelter, medical care and continued attention for animals in its care.

A published goal is not the same as money raised. We have not independently verified current totals or campaign progress, and those numbers can change as December 1 approaches. Check the current official GivingTuesday page for the latest terms and donation route.

How to support the campaign

If you are able to give financially, use the organization's official site and make a contribution appropriate to your circumstances.

If you cannot donate, there are still meaningful ways to help people understand the cause. You could share Rex and Bubba's official stories with someone who cares about rescue, or introduce STAF to a friend in the Cincinnati area who might want to learn about volunteering or adoption. Make clear that you're sharing STAF's campaign in your own voice.

And if you've been thinking about adopting, start with the charity's adoption information, which describes its process. Adoption is a serious commitment, not an impulse response to a seasonal appeal.

A useful lesson for the social-media world

A hashtag such as #JustOneMore is memorable because it points to a real choice. Behind it are people making space and providing care when another animal comes through the door.

Creators who want to share this campaign have a good place to begin: read the original stories, think about the work they represent, and help their audience get to STAF's information. The real stories are compelling enough as they are.
