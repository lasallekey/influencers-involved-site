# Editorial change log: voice and width refinements (PR #9)

Founder-directed refinements applied on top of the conditionally approved batch. **Not published, not authorized.** Facts, citations, external links, titles, meta descriptions, schema and SEO purposes are unchanged; only body copy and the Challenge links below changed.

## What changed in principle

- **Voice:** confident, warm and forward-looking. The Challenge is presented as a worthwhile first step instead of being defended. Repeated "you don't have to / not proof of donation / optional" caveats were removed; the guidance that matters for accuracy (media rights, handler privacy, honest sourcing, likes are not donations) stays, phrased as how to do it well.
- **Central invitation:** "GivingTuesday is December 1, but you don't have to wait until then to show your intention to make a difference. Join the 1% Influence Challenge today." It carries the guides (GT26-01 twice, GT26-02 three times); each charity article closes with its own short, differently worded invitation. All links go to `/challenge/#join`.
- **Kept deliberately:** the editorial-independence note on every article (no affiliation with GivingTuesday or any featured charity), no claim of partnership, no donation guarantee. The one factual statement about the program (pledge to a charity you choose, give directly, certificate recognizes the pledge) is stated once, positively, in GT26-01 and GT26-02.
- **Width:** desktop article column widened from about 68 to about 80 characters per line and centered; title, byline, body, note, breadcrumbs and closing link share one left and right edge (measured at 1024, 1366 and 1920 px). Tables keep their scroll behavior; mobile is unchanged.

Before:

> Ask whether it interests them. Leave them free to participate or not. You don't need a public tagging chain or a demand that friends repost immediately.

**Sharing a charity's campaign: same media-rights guidance, framed as how to do it well.**

Before:

After:

> If a charity has asked supporters to share its campaign, that gives you a sensible starting point. Share it in your own words and with your own images, and use a charity's photos, stories or logos only with its permission.

**Example: removes the "you wouldn't need to claim..." disclaimer.**

Before:

After:

> You'd simply be honest about where your information comes from and let people decide how to help.

**Audience choices: positive framing; the accuracy point (likes are not donations) is kept.**

Before:

> Some people can make a donation; others cannot. Some will share a campaign, volunteer later or quietly save the organization's name. Those are different actions. Don't present likes, views or comments as donations, or assume a post has raised money unless the charity verifies the result.

After:

> Some people will donate, some will share a campaign, and others will volunteer later or quietly save the organization's name. All of those are ways of helping. Just keep the language honest: likes, views and comments aren't donations, and a post hasn't raised money until the charity says so.
